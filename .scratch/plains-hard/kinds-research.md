# Plains Hard: place and kind facts (sources checked 2026-11-22)

The pages are English Wikipedia, fetched 2026-11-22. *Summary only* means the fact came from a summary of a part of the page that was not read word for word. Every game sentence is a draft for teacher review (Q-04).

## New kinds

| Kind | Stored as | Game description (draft) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chalk | rock | A soft, white rock made of the tiny shells of sea plankton. An old sea once covered the Great Plains. | Chalk: "a soft, white, porous, sedimentary carbonate rock"; "tiny fragments of the calcite shells or skeletons of plankton"; "a form of limestone". Niobrara (above) |
| Loess | rock (like peat) | Fine yellow dust carried by the wind and laid down in thick layers. It is not a true rock, but it makes rich farm soil. | Loess: "predominantly silt-sized sediment that is formed by the accumulation of wind-blown dust"; "composed of quartz, feldspar, mica". "Rich farm soil": *teacher check* (not quoted) |
| Flint | rock | A very hard stone made of tiny quartz grains. People made tools from it and struck it to make sparks. | Flint: "a sedimentary cryptocrystalline form of the mineral quartz"; "nodules and masses in sedimentary rocks, such as chalks and limestones"; "widely used to make stone tools and start fires" |

## Dropped or not confirmed (teacher check)

- **Lignite dropped:** the Lignite page calls it "brown coal" "formed from naturally compressed peat", but no source placed it on the West Siberian Plain. H4 reviews peat instead.
- **Kankar not used:** the page never names the Indo-Gangetic Plain. H6 reviews laterite (the Indo-Gangetic page names "laterite deposits").
- **Mongolia** herders, horses, cold winters: *summary only*.
- **Great Plains** wind and wheat: *summary only*. The H1 lesson talks about grass, cattle and the old sea, not wind.
- **Loess "rich farm soil":** a common teaching claim, not quoted.
