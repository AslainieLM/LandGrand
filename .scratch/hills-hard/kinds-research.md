## H1 Tarsier forest, Corella, Bohol

Facts found
- Bohol stratigraphy (Bohol Island Geopark): Alicia Schist (oldest, Late Jurassic to Early Cretaceous), Boctol serpentinite and peridotite (Cretaceous, east Bohol), Wahig Limestone (Early to Middle Miocene), Carmen Formation (Middle Miocene calcareous sediments), Sierra Bullones Limestone, and Maribojoc Limestone (Pliocene reef limestone, west Bohol).
- The Chocolate Hills are eroded marine limestone resting on "hardened clay" (plaque at the Carmen viewing deck, via Wikipedia).
- Gap: I could not read the Corella municipal geologic map, because the server refused the connection. Neighbouring PPDO maps for Sevilla, Catigbian, Baclayon and Lila show Maribojoc Limestone and Carmen Formation. Corella is probably on limestone, but this is not verified.

Candidates
| Kind | New? | Kid line | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| peridotite | new | A dark green rock from deep under the sea floor. | Medium (in Bohol, but on the east side, not in Corella) |
| chert | new | A hard, glassy rock made from tiny sea shells long ago. | Low-Medium (in SE Bohol ophiolite with pillow basalt) |
| limestone | review | Rock made from old coral and shells. Tarsier hills sit on it. | High for Bohol |
| coral stone | review | Old reef rock, like the Maribojoc reef limestone. | Medium |

Sources

## H2 Hills of Antipolo, Rizal (landslide warning signs)

Location answer: Antipolo can be sourced. Keep it as the location, and do not name any disaster event.
- General landslide susceptibility: Victor and Zarco (2018), "Multivariate Logistic Regression Approach for Landslide Susceptibility Assessment of Antipolo, Rizal", Philippine Engineering Journal (UP Diliman). It assesses almost 40% of Antipolo City as potentially dangerous for landslides. It is a city-wide model, not a study of one event. https://journals.upd.edu.ph/index.php/pej/article/view/6550
- MGB geohazard portal (official susceptibility layer, filter to Antipolo): https://experience.arcgis.com/experience/c48f83f81f1548bdb0a76c61638d52d6
- Supporting source: "Barriers to Geohazard Data Integration in Local Development Plans: Evidence from Antipolo City". It says that Antipolo's plans cite MGB hazard maps and No-Build Zones. https://journals.e-palli.com/home/index.php/ajec/article/view/8167 (smaller journal, medium)
- Geology: a study of Rizal aggregates says the quarries in Antipolo, Angono, San Mateo and Rodriguez mine the Montalban Ophiolitic Complex, the Madlum Formation and the Kinabuan Formation. It describes the rock as "predominantly chloritized basalts and andesites with minor clastic rocks and tuffs". https://scibru.fos.ubd.edu.bn/index.php/scibru/article/download/108/49 (Scientia Bruneiana, peer-reviewed; I could not confirm the title or authors because the PDF text was compressed)
- No source found for the "Binangonan limestone" or for "Guadalupe tuff" at Antipolo itself. Peña's Lexicon of Philippine Stratigraphy (2008) and the MGB maps are the next sources to check.

Candidates
| Kind | New? | Kid line | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| chlorite | new | A soft green mineral. It forms when basalt changes over time. | Medium ("chloritized basalts") |
| peridotite | new | Deep sea-floor rock found in old ophiolite hills. | Low (an ophiolite usually has it, but no Antipolo source) |
| basalt | review | Dark rock found in Antipolo's quarries and hills. | High |
| andesite / tuff | review | Hill rocks that can weather into loose soil on slopes. | High / Medium |

## H3 Masungi Georeserve, Baras/Tanay, Rizal

Facts found
- Limestone karst is confirmed. Masungi covers about 2,700 ha of limestone karst in the Upper Marikina Watershed. Its name comes from "masungki" (spiked). Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masungi_Georeserve (weak) and https://masungigeoreserve.com/destination/about-us (operator, not independent).
- In 2023 a UP-NIGS fieldwork team found gastropods and foraminifera in the limestone. Reports call the limestone Paleocene (about 60 million years old), but one report says Eocene, so the age is unresolved. Sources: https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1838571/fossilized-mollusks-in-masungi-geologists-unveil-a-ph-first and https://mb.com.ph/2023/9/29/first-and-oldest-fossil-gastropods-found-in-masungi-georeserve (press, medium)
- Baras municipality says its geology includes andesite and basalt in the hilly areas. I saw this only in a search snippet, because the site did not resolve.
- No source found that names the formation (Kinabuan or Binangonan) at Masungi.

Candidates
| Kind | New? | Kid line | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| limestone | review | Rain slowly melts this rock into sharp spikes. | High |
| calcite | review | The mineral in limestone that rainwater dissolves. | Medium (true of all limestone, but not Masungi-specific) |
| chert | new | Hard rock that rain cannot melt. It is often near limestone. | Low (no Masungi source) |

## H4 Pililla Wind Farm, Rizal

Wind farm facts (sourced)
- 54 MW from 27 Gamesa G90 turbines of 2 MW each, in three clusters. It is in Pililla, Rizal, about 40 km SE of Manila, on hills up to 300 m above sea level. Source: Alternergy, https://www.alternergy.com/pililia-wind-farm (operator)
- Barangay Halayhayin. Completed in June 2015 and launched in January 2016. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pililla_Wind_Farm (weak) and BusinessMirror, https://businessmirror.com.ph/2016/01/20/alternergy-eyeing-more-clean-energy-projects/
- The sources disagree on cost (US$145 million or US$177.9 million), so do not use cost in the game.

Bedrock
- Pililla lies on the eastern (Jalajala peninsula) side of Laguna Caldera. Mount Sembrano, between Jalajala and Pililla, is a basaltic to basaltic-andesite stratovolcano that is older than the caldera. Caldera rocks range from basalt and andesite to dacite, rhyolite and trachyte. Source: Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program, Laguna Caldera (via https://www.volcanodiscovery.com/laguna.html; check against volcano.si.edu)
- No MGB source found for bedrock at the wind farm site itself.

Candidates
| Kind | New? | Kid line | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| dacite | new | Light grey volcano rock, between andesite and rhyolite. | Low-Medium (caldera-wide, not at the site) |
| rhyolite | new | Pale volcano rock full of quartz. | Low-Medium (same) |
| andesite | review | Grey volcano rock from old Mount Sembrano. | Medium |
| basalt | review | Dark lava rock that built the old volcano under the hills. | Medium |

## H5 Clew Bay drumlins, Ireland

Facts found
- Clew Bay has a drumlin field that sea-level rise partly drowned, leaving a "basket of eggs" of small islands. Text is credited to the Geological Survey Ireland. Source: Geological Society 100 Great Geosites, https://www.geolsoc.org.uk/GeositesClewBay. The page gives no count or age.
- Count: the "MAYO GEOLOGY" booklet (adapted from GSI) says "a hundred or so islands", https://www.geoschol.com/counties/MAYO_GEOLOGY.pdf. The often-quoted figure of 365 is folklore: NASA Earth Observatory reports it as local legend, https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/88695/bay-of-the-partly-drowned-hills
- Age: about 20,000 years, from the last ice sheet (NASA Earth Observatory). Clark et al. (2009, GSA Bulletin) date Clew Bay ice-margin features to the Killard Point Stadial. https://mural.maynoothuniversity.ie/1982/1/clark_et_al_gsa_2009.pdf
- Croagh Patrick is mostly metamorphosed Silurian sediments, including quartzite (about 435 million years old). Source: Geological Heritage Sites of Mayo story map, https://storymaps.arcgis.com/stories/02798b0f92014bfba623e76f3a7c8510 (county heritage audit, medium-good). The Clew Bay Complex below it has low-grade metasediments and metamorphosed basic and ultrabasic rocks.

Candidates
| Kind | New? | Kid line | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| quartzite | new | Very hard rock made when sandstone gets squeezed and heated. | High (Croagh Patrick, beside Clew Bay) |
| schist | review | Flaky rock from the old Clew Bay Complex under the hills. | Medium |
| serpentinite | review | Green rock from metamorphosed ultrabasic rock in Clew Bay. | Medium |
| till / erratics | - | Leave out: till is a soil and erratics are a landform. | - |

## H6 Seven Hills of Rome, Italy

Facts found
- Most tuff and pozzolana in Rome came from the Colli Albani volcano, about 25-30 km SE. Ignimbrite on the Capitoline (Rupe Tarpea) and Tufo Lionato (355 ka) came from there. Scoria ("sperone") was used in the Colosseum. Sources: Funiciello and Giordano, "The geology of Rome and urban areas", The Virtual Explorer, https://virtualexplorer.com.au/article/2010/277/the-geology-of-rome-and-urban-areas/resources.html (peer-reviewed). ISPRA Memorie Descrittive vol. 87 (De Rita), https://www.isprambiente.gov.it/contentfiles/00007100/7123-memdes-87-de-rita.pdf (government)
- Travertine is calcium carbonate deposited by springs and is quarried near Tivoli (the Latin name Tibur gives "tiburtinus"). The Colosseum uses travertine for key load-bearing parts. Source: Virtual Explorer, above. "Cappellaccio" is the top layer of travertine, not a volcanic tuff, so do not use it as a tuff name.
- Colli Albani lavas are leucitites rich in leucite. Source: Alex Strekeisen, Alban Hills, https://alexstrekeisen.it/english/provincie/albanhills.php (petrology teaching site, medium) and Mineralogical Magazine, Capo di Bove leucitite, https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/mineralogical-magazine/article/kirschsteinite-in-the-capo-di-bove-melilite-leucitite-lava-cecilite-alban-hills-italy/5DB9BB49C4D232D70A2AB9DBA60FB236

Candidates
| Kind | New? | Kid line | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| travertine | new | Spring water left this rock. Romans built the Colosseum with it. | High |
| leucite | new | White round crystals found in lava from the hills near Rome. | Medium |
| pozzolana | new | Volcanic ash that Romans mixed into strong concrete. | Medium (it is loose, so it is not strictly a rock) |
| tuff | review | Soft volcano-ash rock that the hills of Rome are made of. | High |
| scoria | review | Bubbly lava rock used in the Colosseum. | Medium |

## H7 Guilin karst, China

Facts found
- South China Karst was inscribed in 2007. Guilin was added in the 2014 extension under criteria (vii) and (viii). Source: UNESCO decision, https://whc.unesco.org/en/decisions/6094, and IUCN World Heritage Outlook, https://worldheritageoutlook.iucn.org/node/1147
- Guilin is the type site of continental tower karst (fenglin), with cone karst (fengcong) as well. It formed in a succession of Upper Devonian to Lower Carboniferous limestone about 3,000 m thick, and differential uplift in the Quaternary shaped the towers. Source: IUGS Geoheritage, Guilin Karst, https://iugs-geoheritage.org/?p=10809 (good)
- Dolomite: the IUCN evaluation links dolomite to Shibing, a different part of the property, not to Guilin. The IUGS Guilin page does not mention dolomite.

Candidates
| Kind | New? | Kid line | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| dolomite | new | A rock like limestone, but tougher and slower to dissolve. | Low for Guilin (Medium for South China Karst as a whole) |
| limestone | review | Rain dissolved this rock into tall tower hills. | High |
| calcite | review | The mineral that makes up limestone and cave crystals. | Medium |
| dripstone | review | Cave rock in Guilin's tower caves. | Low-Medium (caves are documented, the kind is not) |
