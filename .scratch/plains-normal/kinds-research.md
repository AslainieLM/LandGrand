# Plains Normal: place and kind facts (sources checked 2026-11-22)

The pages are English Wikipedia, fetched 2026-11-22. *Unread* means the fact came only from a summary of a page part that was not read verbatim. Every game sentence is a draft for teacher review (Q-04).

## Places

| Fact used | Source and supporting words |
|---|---|
| Candaba Swamp is a Pampanga wetland, flooded in the wet season and farmed in the dry season | Candaba Swamp: about 32,000 ha of "freshwater ponds, swamps and marshes"; "submerged underwater" in the wet season; farmland (watermelon, rice) from November to April |
| Wild ducks rest there | Candaba (town): "Migrant wild ducks" use it as a sanctuary (Philippine duck, mallard, gadwall and others) |
| Candaba sediment is rich in plant matter | Candaba (town): "sustained deposits of humus and decaying plant residues" |
| Pinatubo erupted in 1991 | 1991 eruption of Mount Pinatubo: activity began on April 2; the main eruption was June 15 |
| Lahars are volcanic mudflows | Lahar: a "violent type of mudflow or debris flow" |
| Lahar mud covered towns and farms | Lahar: over 6 m of mud covered Porac, Mabalacat, Tarlac City, Capas, Concepcion and Bamban. 1991 eruption: "Several lowland towns were flooded or partially buried in mud"; damage to "cropland" |
| Pinatubo's rocks are andesite and dacite | Mount Pinatubo: "an andesite and dacite stratovolcano" — *unread*; the USGS pages were blocked (HTTP 403). **Teacher check.** |
| Deltas form where a river meets the sea | River delta: deltas form "when a river carrying sediment reaches a body of water" |
| The Agno built the Pangasinan Plain; gold is panned along it | Agno River: "a vast alluvial fan and delta called the Pangasinan Plain"; gold is panned from Kabayan to San Manuel |
| Agusan Marsh: Mindanao, protected, floating homes, Lolong | Agusan Marsh Wildlife Sanctuary: Agusan del Sur; Ramsar site 1009 (1999); Manobos "living on floating homes"; the sanctuary "was home to" Lolong |
| The Mekong Delta is in southern Vietnam and built from river sediment | Mekong Delta: in "southern Vietnam"; "flat flood plains"; the soil is "mainly … sediment from the Mekong" |
| People sell fruit from boats there | Cái Răng Floating Market: vendors "sell fresh fruits, vegetables, and local products directly from their boats" |

## New kinds

| Kind | Game description | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Peat (stored as a rock) | Soft brown matter made of old, wet plants. It is not a true rock yet, but in time it can become coal. | Peat: "an accumulation of partially decayed vegetation" in wetlands; "often the first step" toward coal. The page does not call it a rock. Lignite: "sedimentary rock" formed from "naturally compressed peat" |
| Laterite (rock) | A red, iron-rich rock or soil of hot, wet places. People cut it into building blocks. | Laterite: a soil "rich in iron and aluminum" of "hot, wet areas"; "considered a rock type as well as a soil type"; cut into bricks for Angkor temple enclosures |

## Not confirmed (teacher check)

- That the swamp material at Candaba or Agusan is peat. The pages say "humus and decaying plant residues" and "swamp forests".
- That Pinatubo's rocks are andesite and dacite (from a summary only).
- That laterite occurs in the Mekong Delta itself. The source says only "hot, wet areas" and gives Cambodian temples as the example. The N5 lesson therefore says "of hot, wet lands" and does not say "in the Mekong".
