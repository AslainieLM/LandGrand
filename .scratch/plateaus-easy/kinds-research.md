## E1 Bukidnon Plateau
- Plateau def (general knowledge, not fetched): high, fairly flat land with steep sides. UNCONFIRMED by source.
- Elevation: province average 915 m (S1). S2: high volcanic plains/terraces around Kitanglad/Kalatungan at 600-1,200 m. S1: "Bukidnon is a landlocked plateau", "only plateau in Northern Mindanao"; southern/eastern edges mountainous.
- Formed: S1 "mainly a volcanic zone of pyroclastic, basaltic, and andesitic cones." S2 "The whole of Bukidnon is of volcanic origin, except for the southern part"; "Volcanic mudflows or lahars from Mt. Kitanglad and Mt. Kalatungan underlie the greater part of the Bukidnon Plateau."
- Rocks (S2): Kalatungan "fresh calc-alkaline basalt"; Kitanglad "shoshonitic basalt ... and andesite".
- Soil minerals (S2, Manupali slope): clay minerals "halloysite, gibbsite, goethite, hematite and cristobalite"; Ultisol/Oxisol at lower elevations.
- Farms (S1): rice, corn; plantations of pineapples, bananas, sugarcane; Del Monte Philippines (DMPI), Lapanday, Mt. Kitanglad Agri-Development Corp grow pineapple.
- Kid facts: Mt. Kitanglad is an extinct volcano, 2,899 m, home of the Philippine eagle (S1). Rivers Cagayan, Pulangi, Tagoloan cut deep canyons into the plateau (S1).

## E2 Lanao Plateau and Lake Lanao
- Elevation: lake ~700 m (S3, "700 meter elevation"). A search snippet put the lake in "Bukidnon-Lanao Plateau" at ~702 m (not opened, UNCONFIRMED).
- Formed: S3 and S4 "tectonic-volcanic damming of a basin between two mountain ranges, and the collapse of a large volcano." A Scribd report (not opened) says depressed fault blocks from magma movement. Age estimates vary 2-20 million yr (search snippet only).
- NOT confirmed: "plateau built by lava". S3/S4 say damming + volcano collapse, not lava-built plateau. Rock types at Lanao: no page gave any. Do not claim basalt/tuff AT Lanao from a source; S5 basalt is at the outlet (see E3). Region-level link: S1/S2 volcanic Bukidnon-Lanao highland (S2 table names "Lanao-Bukidnon Highland" via BSWM per search snippet only).
- Kid facts: 2nd largest lake in PH after Laguna de Bay; counted among 15 "ancient lakes"; max depth 122 m, mean 60.3 m (S4). Only outlet is Agus River (S3). Mt. Ragang 2,815 m highest point of Lanao del Sur (S7; S7 says it is a volcano? NOT stated, UNCONFIRMED).

## E3 Maria Cristina Falls, Agus River (Iligan)
- Height 98 m (S6), ~9.3 km SW of Iligan City, Lanao del Norte. Twin falls: "flow is separated by a rock at the brink" (S6). Other channel feeds Tinago Falls (S3).
- River (S5): rises at Lake Lanao (700 m), "flows over a basalt rock formation", descends ~2,200 ft (670 m) to Iligan Bay; canyon "suggests a short erosional period".
- Hydropower: Agus VI plant powered by the falls, 200 MW potential, ~130 m3/s, NAPOCOR, commissioned 31 May 1953 (S6). Agus system "generates 70% of the electricity used in Mindanao" (S3/S5; stated as of article, date unknown).
- "Falls drop off plateau edge": S6 does NOT mention plateau. Inference only (lake 700 m -> sea 0 m, S5). UNCONFIRMED as a geology claim.
- Kid facts: nicknamed "twin falls"; lights Mindanao.

## Suggested NEW kinds (all sourced in S2, Manupali slope, Bukidnon)
1. halloysite: clay mineral in Bukidnon volcanic soil (S2). Kid hook: a clay made when volcanic rock rots.
2. gibbsite: aluminium mineral in same soils (S2); main bauxite mineral (general knowledge, UNCONFIRMED here).
3. goethite: iron mineral in same soils (S2); alternative to limonite.
4. cristobalite: silica mineral in same soils (S2). Weak for Grade 5.
NOT recommended: diatomite, augite, plagioclase, travertine at falls (no source found for any in these places). Columnar basalt not a kind.
