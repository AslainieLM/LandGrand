## N1 Table Mountain, South Africa
- Elevation: about 1,085 m (Maclear's Beacon; infobox 1,084.6 m, text 1,086 m). Rises about 1,000 m above the city.
- Formed: a syncline, once a valley floor; the anticline beside it eroded away. Flat top survives thanks to the "extremely hard quartzitic sandstone" of Table Mountain Sandstone (TMS); softer layers above (Pakhuis tillite) eroded.
- Source https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_Mountain:
  - TMS: "highly resistant to erosion"; upper ~600 m; Ordovician, about 450-510 Ma.
  - Cape Granite: basement, late Precambrian, "not nearly as resistant to weathering"; seen at Lion's Head and The Boulders; weathered granite soil is more fertile (vineyards).
  - Malmesbury: "heavily folded and altered phyllites and hornfelses", "known informally as the Malmesbury shales".
  - Graafwater Formation: 70 m of maroon mudstones under TMS (alternate kind: mudstone).
- Kid facts: (1) the flat top is hard sandstone that wind and rain could not wear down while softer rock around it washed away; (2) the rock is older than the dinosaurs, 450 million years or more.
- Unconfirmed: no mineral names on the page. "Quartzitic" is an adjective, not a formal rock name. Malmesbury is shale only informally (really phyllite/hornfels). Page read only to char 100000 of 208151.

## N2 Monument Valley, USA
- Elevation: valley floor "5,000 to 6,000 ft" (1,500-1,800 m); largest buttes rise "1,000 ft (300 m) above the valley floor".
- Formed: rivers carved the valley; hard cap rock leaves mesas and buttes, erosion shrinks them to remnants like Totem Pole.
- Source https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_Valley:
  - Floor is "mostly siltstone of the Cutler Group" (or sand weathered from it).
  - Buttes: Organ Rock Shale (bottom), de Chelly Sandstone (middle), Moenkopi Formation capped by Shinarump Conglomerate.
  - "vivid red coloration comes from iron oxide"; blue-gray rocks from manganese oxide.
  - Uranium mined 1945-1967 in Shinarump areas; vanadium and copper associated.
- Kid facts: (1) the red color is rust-like iron oxide in the rock; (2) the giant buttes are what is left after the land around them eroded.
- Unconfirmed: page says "iron oxide", never "hematite" (inference, flag B). No per-layer elevations. Wikipedia "De Chelly Sandstone" page returned content about the Yeso Group with nothing on color or Monument Valley; do not cite it for N2.

## N3 Grand Canyon, Colorado Plateau
- Elevation: river about 2,600 ft (800 m); Phantom Ranch 2,500 ft (760 m); North Rim averages about 8,000 ft (2,400 m).
- Formed: Colorado River cut down through the rising plateau, exposing layers.
- Source https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Canyon:
  - "the 270-million-year-old Kaibab Limestone" at the rim.
  - Coconino Sandstone: "abundant geological evidence of aeolian sand dune deposition".
  - Redwall Limestone: named with the karst R-aquifer; no age given.
  - "the two-billion-year-old Vishnu Schist": oldest exposed rock.
  - Volcanic rocks are the youngest (about 100,000 to 3 million years); type not named.
  - Calcite named (cave deposits).
- NPS https://www.nps.gov/grca/learn/nature/geologicformations.htm: "Harder, erosion-resistant rocks such as the Coconino Sandstone and the Redwall Limestone have eroded into bold cliffs"; granite gorges metamorphosed about 1,750 Ma.
- Chert (for NEW chert): archived NPS table (per search summary) lists Kaibab limestone/dolomite "contains chert nodules" and Redwall "white chert lenses": https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20121024202310/http:/www.nature.nps.gov/geology/education/Foos/grand.pdf (not opened; exact phrase unverified).
- Kid facts: (1) the Coconino layer is an ancient desert's sand dunes turned to stone; (2) the bottom schist is about 2 billion years old.
- Unconfirmed: Zoroaster Granite is not on any page I read; a search summary calls it granite and granodiorite, about 1,750-1,680 Ma (https://web.uri.edu/geofieldtrip/?p=46, unopened). Basalt not named (flag C). Redwall iron-oxide red stain not found. Page read only to char 100000 of 259633.

## N4 Mount Roraima, Venezuela
- Elevation: 2,810 m (Venezuela side); Guyana high point 2,772 m. Cliffs rise about 400-1,000 m.
- Formed: flat-topped tepui in the Pacaraima chain; old sandstone plateau left standing as erosion removed the land around it; rain shapes caves (network over 15 km).
- Source https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Roraima:
  - "Proterozoic age sandstone formed about 1.7 to 2 billion years ago"; on a "granite and gneiss base".
  - Rock "contains a large amount of quartz deposits"; 98% silica; "white or pink crystals several centimeters long"; Raleigh called it a "crystal mountain".
  - Soil "highly acidic, poor in nutrients".
- Quartzite: per search summaries, Wray (https://rd.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-90-481-3055-9_9, "The Gran Sabana: The World's Finest Quartzite Karst?") calls tepuis Precambrian Roraima Group quartzites and sandstones; quartzite = quartz-arenite (https://laventa.it/en/projects/46-tepui/468-tepuiquarziti). Exact phrases unverified by me.
- Kid facts: (1) the top is a cliff-edged "lost world" with rock about 2 billion years old; (2) so much quartz that explorers called it a crystal mountain.
- Unconfirmed: Wikipedia Roraima page names no quartzite or Roraima Group; Angel Falls appears only as a See-also link (it falls from Auyan-tepui, not Roraima; I did not source that), so do not tie it to Roraima geology; jasper, gold, diamond not found (do not use). Roraima Supergroup and Formation Wikipedia URLs returned 404.

## N5 Altiplano and Salar de Uyuni, Bolivia
- Elevation: Altiplano "averages about 3,750 meters"; Uyuni 3,650-3,663 m (page gives three values).
- Formed: Altiplano is a high basin between Andean ranges (origin debated, five hypotheses). Uyuni came from "seven Late Pleistocene lakes" (Lake Minchin, Lake Tauca) that dried out.
- Source https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salar_de_Uyuni:
  - Brine is "a saturated solution of sodium chloride, lithium chloride, and magnesium chloride"; "Lithium is concentrated in the brine under the salt crust"; "porous halite body lying under the brine"; potassium chloride; "as well as borax".
  - "covered by an 8-meter-thick layer of salt" (lead); geology section says tens of cm to a few m.
  - Altiplano page: Uyuni and Coipasa "formed after the Altiplano paleolakes dried out".
- Volcanic, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altiplano%E2%80%93Puna_volcanic_complex: "The APVC is the largest ignimbrite province of the Neogene"; "andesite as the major product"; "spans the countries of Argentina, Bolivia, and Chile".
- Gypsum and ulexite: search summary of a USGS report (ulexite ore mineral, gypsum gangue): https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1995/0618/report.pdf (403 to me) or https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1988/0210/report.pdf; which report says what is unknown.
- Kid facts: (1) Uyuni is the world's largest salt flat, an ancient lake that dried up; (2) lithium for batteries lies in the salty water under the crust.
- Unconfirmed: gypsum, ulexite, ignimbrite and tuff are NOT on the Uyuni Wikipedia page. That Uyuni itself sits on ignimbrite is only a USGS figure caption seen in a search summary ("bounded by an ignimbrite plateau"). Tuff at Uyuni is unsourced, so tuff is not proposed. No lithium mineral sourced (do not use lepidolite, spodumene). Altiplano page does not name tin or silver. Salt thickness sources conflict.
