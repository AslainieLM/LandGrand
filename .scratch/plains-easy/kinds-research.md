# Plains Easy: kinds and place facts (sources checked 2026-11-21)

All pages are English Wikipedia, fetched 2026-11-21. Several pages were long and only partly read. Where a fact rests only on a page summary, it is marked *unread*. Every game sentence is still a draft for teacher review (Q-04).

## Places

| Fact used in the game | Source and supporting words |
|---|---|
| Central Luzon has the largest plain in the Philippines | Central Luzon: "contains the largest plain in the country" |
| Nueva Ecija is the "Rice Granary of the Philippines" | Central Luzon and Nueva Ecija pages: "Rice Granary of the Philippines"; Nueva Ecija has the largest rice yield |
| Pangasinan means "place of salt" | Pangasinan: "place of salt" or "place of salt-making", from "asin"; "a major producer of salt in the Philippines" |
| Salt is made from sea water in ponds | Pangasinan: the coastal industry is salt-making. The evaporation-pond detail is *unread* (summary only); Halite supports evaporation of seawater |
| The Cagayan is the longest river in the Philippines | Cagayan River: "Longest river in the Philippines", about 505 km |
| It floods and has an alluvial plain in Isabela | Cagayan River: "subject to extensive flooding"; "extensive flood plain"; an alluvial plain from Ilagan to Tumauini |
| Limestone in the river plain (E3 pickup) | Cagayan River: tributaries deposited "mostly limestone sands and clays" across the valley |
| Floodplain soil is clay, silt, sand and gravel | Floodplain: soils "usually consist of clays, silts, sands, and gravels deposited during floods" |
| What a plain is | Plain: "a flat expanse of land that generally has few rapid or steep changes in elevation"; plains form from sediment deposited "by water, ice, or wind", from "flowing lava", or by erosion |

**Not confirmed:**
- That Isabela grows mostly corn. The props show corn, but the lesson says only "crops".
- That the Pangasinan beds are "salt beds" by name. The game uses it as a plain description.
- Whether Central Luzon soil is clay or silt. The Central Luzon pages did not say; the claim rests on the general Floodplain page.

## New kinds

| Kind | Game description | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Siltstone (rock) | A smooth rock made of silt, finer than sand. Silt settles in quiet water. | Siltstone: "composed mostly of silt"; forms in "relatively quiet depositional environments" |
| Halite (mineral) | Rock salt. It forms when salty sea or lake water dries up. | Halite: "commonly known as rock salt"; "has formed from the evaporation of seawater or salty lake water" |
| Gypsum (mineral) | A very soft white mineral left by drying sea water. It is used for plaster and chalk. | Gypsum: Mohs 2, "a soft sulfate mineral"; "deposited from lake and sea water"; "forms as an evaporite mineral"; plaster, "blackboard or sidewalk chalk" |
| Mudstone (rock) | A fine rock made from hardened mud and clay. | Mudstone: "fine-grained sedimentary rock whose original constituents were clays or muds" |

## Shared kinds

These keep their existing text: clay, sandstone, shale, quartz, conglomerate and limestone. The review sentences in the spec use general facts:
- Sand hardens into sandstone.
- Silt hardens into siltstone.
- River gravel hardens into conglomerate.
- Beach sand is mostly quartz (this is the Hills E3 source, Britannica "sand dune").

The teacher should check every sentence.
